The math

Two evidence points, one transparent model

The calculator treats proficiency as a multiplier on the productivity value already created by AI coding tools.

Start with the external productivity benchmark

Three randomized field experiments involving 4,867 developers reported a 26.08% increase in completed tasks among developers with access to an AI coding assistant.

Base productivity lift = 26.08%

Apply the observed proficiency improvement

Andela 101 learners improved from 69.1% to 80.7% on an agentic proficiency assessment—a 16.8% relative increase.

26.08% × 1.168 = 30.46%

Value only the incremental capacity

The model values the 4.38-point difference, then counts the selected share as usable engineering capacity and subtracts the total program investment.

Engineers × loaded cost × 4.38% × capture rate

Research foundation

Built from observed results, with the model clearly labeled

Five studies help establish the productivity opportunity, the role of training, and why results differ across teams and experience levels.

Primary calculator input 26.08%

Enterprise developers completed more tasks with an AI coding assistant

Three randomized field experiments spanning 4,867 software developers produced the pooled benchmark used by this calculator. Results varied across participating companies, so the number is best treated as a scenario baseline rather than a guarantee.

Cui et al. · Read the study
Training and adoption 26% → 41%

Brief training increased productive LLM adoption

In a randomized legal-analysis study, approximately ten minutes of training increased voluntary adoption and improved performance relative to untrained access. The domain differs, but the study supports a practical distinction between providing a tool and enabling its productive use.

Chen & Bao · Read the study
Experience matters +14% / +34%

Average gains were larger for novice workers

Across 5,179 customer-support agents, generative AI increased productivity by 14% on average and 34% among novice and lower-skilled workers, with comparatively little improvement among the most experienced workers.

Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond · Read the study
Developer field evidence +55%

Code output increased in an enterprise quasi-experiment

A study at Ant Group found more than 50% higher code output among programmers using an AI coding assistant. Statistically significant gains were concentrated among junior developers and the outcome measured code output rather than delivered business value.

Gambacorta et al. · Read the study
Controlled coding task 55.8% faster

Professional developers completed a coding task faster

In a randomized experiment with 95 professional developers, participants using GitHub Copilot completed a standardized JavaScript task 55.8% faster. The study examined one constrained task and found larger benefits among less-experienced developers.

Peng et al. · Read the study

Methodology

Transparent by design

The cited studies measure different populations and outcomes. They are presented as supporting evidence, not averaged together. The calculator uses only Cui et al.'s pooled 26.08% developer benchmark as its external productivity input.

What this estimate means

This is an illustrative business-case model, not a measured production outcome. The calculator assumes that the relative proficiency improvement translates proportionally into increased productivity value from AI tools. Actual results vary by team, work type, adoption, and implementation quality.

Andela 101 uses the observed 16.8% relative learning improvement. Andela 201 uses a 33.6% planning assumption.

No reduction in headcount is implied. “Value” represents capacity that an organization may redirect toward higher-value engineering work.
